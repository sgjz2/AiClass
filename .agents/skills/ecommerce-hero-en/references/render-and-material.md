# Light, material and spatial integration

Purpose: develop photographic/rendered finish and diagnose pasted-on integration, excessive reflections and texture. Read for dimensional artwork or related refinement. Optional examples appear in [visual forms](visual-form-reference.md#light-and-material).

## Reduce decorative gradients

Reduce all-over multicolor soft gradients, colored halos without sources, and simultaneous gradients on product, title and ornament. Prefer clean solid-color space, bounded color fields, actual scenes or justified materials. Use shadows, overlap, tone and spatial composition for depth. Backgrounds need not be blank or uniformly minimal.

Preserve natural continuous shading from curved surfaces, environmental reflection and depth of field. If a gradient serves emission or depth, concentrate it in the relevant region with a clear light source or compositional role; avoid washed-out/fog-filtered images. Borrow layering from older gradient references without automatically reusing their color effects.

## Explain form through light

Choose key, fill, environmental reflection and tonal distribution by product shape and scene. Highlights describe contour, turns and materials; shadows retain appropriate depth. Soft/hard/multiple lights, strong reflection and dramatic illumination are all available.

Judge product, backdrop, support surface, type and ornament together. Local strong light can coexist with broad quiet surfaces. Recede secondary elements through area, contrast, clarity or reflection so highlights concentrate without fragmenting the form.

## Treat surfaces by real materials

Use confirmed source materials to set highlight shape, width, intensity, reflection sharpness and texture. Gloss plastic, rough surfaces, glass, metal and fabric have suitable responses; similar materials may respond similarly.

When composing a real product layer, match scene viewpoint/light to the source. If relighting is allowed, share coherent illumination while preserving construction and marks. Check support, hand contact, perspective, edges, overlap and depth of field; conceptual suspension follows its selected spatial logic and shadow/depth relationships.

Select generation, editing or composition through [production routes](production.md#select-the-production-route), achieving identity and integration together. Retain valid lighting. Local logo/construction repairs do not automatically replace the whole subject. An asset compositor positions assets; it does not relight them or solve reflections/contact shadows. Source pixels alone do not establish visual success.

## Allocate texture and color intensity

Let the complete form read first, then reveal appropriate detail. Distribute texture contrast/sharpness by focus, distance, light and material; keep key structures, patterns and inscriptions accurate and legible.

Saturation, gloss and warm/cool contrast can all work. Judge them together with brightness and texture. At full size inspect repeating grain, harsh micro-contrast and excessively bright edges; at display size check whether form or fragmented bright surfaces read first.

## Detail hierarchy and credibility

Derive seams, edge transitions, controls, fabric weave and print from visible product evidence. Do not invent screws, holes, construction or inscriptions. Contact shadows, controlled reflections, occlusion and surface materials can provide spatial detail. Thematic ornament may add local rhythm or beauty.

Concentrate richer detail near focus, keeping broad surfaces and copy regions quiet. Weave, grain and brushing need appropriate scale, direction and density instead of identical sharp microtexture everywhere. Background depth-of-field blur is allowed while identity remains clear. Hard texture, white sharpening rings, metallic plastic and noise are not clarity improvements.

## Refine actual gaps

Locate competition and adjust local reflection, texture, light or focus. Retain successful color/tension and verify identity details. State the main light/material relationship and let the model coordinate other parameters. Review actual exports through [acceptance](production.md#review-and-delivery), checking revised space, highlight priorities and surfaces.

## Local separation in dark or shared-hue scenes

Narrow rim light, broad reflections and contact brightness can separate dark products. Highlights should describe form rather than uniformly trace it. Teasers may withhold detail; normal sales imagery must retain key structures. Shared-hue product, platform and background can separate by tone, roughness and occlusion without saturated highlights everywhere.

Reflections beside emissive screens should continue the source's color, direction and distance falloff. Creative lighting does not prove actual performance. Broad light bands can coexist with low-contrast fabric; see [L1-01–L1-05](lighting-and-perspective-study.md#light-and-materials).

## Calibrate the role of light against references

L1-01 separates a dark subject with limited rim/table light; L1-02 lets emissive content shape environmental reflection; L1-03 separates one hue with tone/material; L1-04 contrasts continuous bands and mesh; L1-05 shows thinness with edges, local contact light and darker rear planes. Select a relationship matching actual materials, then tune intensity.

Reducing decorative gradients preserves natural tonal transitions and local backlight with a spatial purpose. Do not flatten volume merely to obtain solid colors. Dark references do not require crushed product blacks; retain structure according to teaser/sales needs. Avoid identical gloss on every surface and uniform sharpening masquerading as material detail.
