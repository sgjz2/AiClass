# Visual development and finishing

Describe a visible relationship rather than adjectives: which action, scale, spatial, material or letterform relationship communicates which evidenced main message? Inspect real references by role: identity, composition, light, type/decoration or commercial groups. Transfer mechanisms across categories without copying claims or props. Respect the user’s explicit positive/negative labels in the index; an isolated useful mechanism cannot turn a negative image into a quality benchmark.

Specify product scale, camera angle, overlap, reading path and support/conceptual suspension; key light and material response; letterform character and relationship to product direction; main/supporting/transaction groups and terms; the role of each decoration. Rich information can be organized. Many equally strong focal points require restructuring. Gentle light and small type are not universal defaults.

Examples from the atlas: G5-04 uses a strong offer heading while products remain identifiable; G6-04 demonstrates an action; G7-07 separates dark material through light and mesh; G2-06 uses large type and shoe overlap in a graphic environment. Select mechanisms instead of stacking effects.

Compare a few expressive mechanisms when direction is open; retain a successful baseline when it is chosen. Develop type, scene and product together with [generative typography](generative-typography.md), then inspect identity and facts. Authentic product layers require real integration work; dense copy does not automatically require a text-free image with plain-font overlays.

When the user identifies pasted-on integration or requests render quality, inspect actual rendering references and map key/fill positions, highlight width for each material, support/contact, cast-shadow direction, and background depth into the generation prompt. Borrowing only palette or a pedestal does not transfer rendering quality. Identity preservation does not require frozen source lighting: when geometry, color, structure and marks must match but exact source pixels are not required, consider unified product/environment relighting followed by identity inspection and original-mark correction. Do not re-overlay the old cutout after relighting and reintroduce the same mismatch. If exact pixels are required, match the environment to the source camera/light and acknowledge any material limitation. Establish volume and distinct material responses before typography; evaluate contact, cast shadow and environment bounce together. Grain, glow, leaves or decorative strips do not repair missing volume. This is a conditional route, not a universal dark-studio or curved-pedestal template.

Bland work may need a clearer value relationship, letterform energy, product scale, material contrast or offer group. Overprocessed work may have false highlights, uniform plastic surfaces, oversharpening, arbitrary props or too many leaders. Do not respond only by adding effects or reducing saturation. Preserve useful cropping, rhythm, contrast and action.

Local repairs preserve good layout, lettering, identity and accurate copy. Edit a spelling error locally before resorting to a style-matched overlay. Rebuild only when the concept or composition fails, rather than randomly regenerating the whole image.

Resolve identity/fact blockers, then review theme, hierarchy, type/decoration, transactions, space and materials. Realistic work needs correct contacts and shadows; conceptual suspension needs coherent projection and overlap. Full-size inspection covers detail, small-size inspection covers focal hierarchy and readability. Brand KVs need not have prices/buttons; promotions must retain required terms.

Compare actual reference mechanisms with concrete gaps. The atlas's G1 examples distinguish clutter, pasted-on integration and detached product scale. Pixel fidelity is not visual quality and generated atmosphere is not performance evidence. Previous case runs are experience, not controlled skill comparisons or conversion data.
